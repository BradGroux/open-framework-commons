# Shared Principles

These principles are the common foundation of the Open Framework Ecosystem. Each product expresses them in its own context.

## People first

People supply purpose, judgment, responsibility, relationships, and the knowledge that makes work meaningful. AI and automation may assist people; they do not erase human accountability.

## Own the method, rent the tool

Methods and principles should survive changes in models, vendors, applications, and technical stacks. Tools are replaceable. The documented way of thinking and working is the durable asset.

## Play the long game

Prefer durable relationships, useful work, shared learning, and ecosystem health over short-term attention or transactions.

## Contribute before extracting

Create value, listen, and learn before asking for attention, access, or commercial return. Contribution is not a disguised obligation or sales tactic.

## Steward what matters

Leave frameworks, relationships, communities, and shared work stronger than you found them. Preserve context, commitments, decisions, and learning so others can continue responsibly.

## Keep products independent

Shared philosophy does not require a shared audience, method, repository shape, release schedule, or implementation. People should be able to choose the product that fits their needs.

## Build in the open

Make principles, reasoning, examples, limits, and changes visible. Welcome useful contribution while keeping authority and product boundaries clear.

## Learn honestly

Distinguish experience, community feedback, formal research, and real-world application. Do not present an idea, example, or review as stronger evidence than it is.

## Use technology as an amplifier

Technology should help people apply sound methods. It should not become the foundation, the point of the framework, or a shortcut around judgment.
