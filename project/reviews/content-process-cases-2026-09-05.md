## Worked conflict and partial adoption

Fictional Product A adopts an exact published Commons edition, but a local publishing instruction appears to require a private example to be shared. Its maintainer pauses that publication only and records both statements and the adopted identity. Other authorized work continues. The product owner checks the privacy constraint; the Commons steward may explain shared meaning but does not authorize the private disclosure. The owner narrows the local instruction to permitted summaries, records the rationale and any dissent, and permits only publication within that decision. If rights remain unknown, publication stays deferred with an owner and a trigger: new permission or a reviewed safe summary. Silence is not approval.

If instead Product A intends a substantive local exception, it states that it adopts the named edition **with the specified deviation**, identifies affected guidance, rationale and accountable authority, and links the local decision. That is neither complete agreement nor certification; it does not amend Commons or automatically remove the product from scope. Commons recognition and product adoption remain separate decisions.

## Compatibility decisions exercised

A spelling fix that preserves reader choices is editorial. An optional example preserving those choices is a compatible addition. Rewording “contribute before asking” so legitimate help need not be earned is a substantive clarification because some prior interpretations change. A new required obligation or authority transfer is potentially incompatible even if the diff is one sentence. The date on the edition cannot decide any of these cases.

Decision 0001’s v1.1.0 scope addition remains its historical compatible-addition decision. Four products currently record v1.1.0 adoption and AI Dev Days retains v1.0.0; that difference alone establishes no failure. This review changes no adoption record.

## Result and limits

The revised text supplies a bounded outcome in these cases without a shared implementation or product workflow. The deferred journal proposal tests counterevidence rather than assuming all shared-looking ideas should be promoted. Actual usability and effectiveness require product-owned observation and feedback. Revisit this assessment when real use exposes a different interpretation or outcome; do not convert these fictional cases into claimed results.
